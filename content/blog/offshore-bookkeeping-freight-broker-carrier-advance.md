Published October 5, 2026. This article covers bookkeeping workflow support, not accounting, tax, legal, transportation, insurance, sanctions, or payment advice.

## An advance is a load-level open item

Freight brokers sometimes advance fuel or other approved amounts before a load is delivered. The cash leaves before the final carrier settlement is ready, so it cannot be controlled only through the weekly payment run. If an advance is posted straight to carrier cost, the team may pay the full agreed amount again at settlement or leave an abandoned load advance buried in expense. The safer model treats the advance as an open item tied to a specific load, carrier, authorization, and expected clearing event.

The advance register begins with load ID, carrier legal name and approved ID, rate confirmation version, origin, destination, pickup date, expected delivery, gross carrier amount, advance purpose, requested amount, fee, payment method, approver, and bank reference. Store the carrier's approved payment destination in the controlled vendor master rather than copying details from an email. A changed destination is a separate verification event, not an update the preparer makes while processing the advance.

## Establish the evidence gate before cash moves

Define what an advance request must include. Common elements are an executed rate confirmation, dispatch assignment, pickup or loaded status, named requester, approved amount or percentage, carrier identity verification, and authorization under the company's limit matrix. The preparer checks presence and consistency; the authorized operations or finance person decides whether the request qualifies. Urgency is not a substitute for that decision trail.

The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration provides [broker registration information](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/broker-registration) and other carrier resources. Use current FMCSA information where it is relevant to the company's process, while recognizing that registration data does not prove the legitimacy of a particular payment request. The business should define its own fraud, insurance, contracting, and payment controls with qualified advice.

## Carry the advance into final settlement

Once delivery evidence arrives, build the carrier settlement from the approved gross amount. Show accessorials, deductions, prior advances, advance fees, claims holds under approved policy, and final amount due on separate lines. The settlement must reference the original bank payment and advance register entry. Do not rely on matching carrier name and amount because a carrier can have several loads with similar economics on the same route.

Reconcile in both directions. Every paid advance must appear in the open-item register, and every register item marked paid must trace to bank activity. Every delivered load with an advance must show that advance on its final settlement, and every settlement deduction labeled “advance” must trace back to an actual disbursement. This four-way check catches duplicates, missing deductions, and deductions assigned to the wrong load.

## Follow a reassigned load without losing the money trail

Load 78142 has a $4,800 approved carrier rate. Carrier A receives a $1,000 fuel advance after pickup, then suffers a mechanical failure. Operations approves a transload and Carrier B completes the final leg for $2,100. The register does not transfer Carrier A's $1,000 to Carrier B. It leaves the original cash with Carrier A's load segment, records the operational resolution, and separately supports Carrier B's settlement.

If Carrier A is ultimately entitled to $2,700 for the completed segment, its settlement shows $2,700 gross less the $1,000 advance and any separately approved fee. Carrier B's $2,100 settlement has no advance deduction. If the parties dispute the split, bookkeeping leaves the amounts open and presents the documents and arithmetic. Operations and authorized finance owners decide the contractual outcome.

## Age by expected clearing event

Not all old advances indicate the same problem. Classify open items as in transit, delivered awaiting proof, proof received awaiting settlement, canceled, reassigned, disputed, duplicate risk, payment returned, or recovery required. Use the expected delivery date and final-settlement date to measure age. An advance two days old on a long-haul load may be ordinary; an advance one day old on a load canceled before pickup may need immediate action.

Exception notes should identify the observed conflict and requested decision. For example: “Bank trace confirms $850 paid to carrier ID 918 on load 78091, but the transportation system shows the load canceled before pickup; operations owner to confirm recovery action.” Avoid unverified accusations. Keep emails, call notes, and platform screenshots in approved storage with timestamps and source identities.

## Respect payment and identity boundaries

An offshore bookkeeper can compile requests, compare the rate confirmation with the transportation record, update the register, reperform settlement math, and draft a payment file for review. The role should not create and approve a carrier, change bank details, release cash, settle a dispute, or decide a fraud response. Separate access so the same account cannot modify master data and complete payment approval.

## Reconcile advance fees and returned payments

Some payment services deduct an advance fee before the carrier receives cash; others debit the broker for the gross amount and bill the fee separately. Store gross authorized advance, fee payer, fee amount, net carrier receipt, and bank debit in distinct fields. The rate confirmation and approved policy determine what may appear on final settlement. A bookkeeper should not assume the carrier bears a fee merely because it is visible in the platform.

If a payment rejects or returns, keep the original attempt and bank reversal linked to the load. A second payment requires a new trace and approval while the first remains in returned status. This prevents the retry from looking like a duplicate and allows the reviewer to confirm that only one payment ultimately reached the carrier.

At close, tie uncleared advances to the relevant balance-sheet or clearing account under the approved accounting policy, cleared advances to carrier settlements, fees to the designated account, and payments to the bank. Review negative or over-cleared loads, advances older than the threshold, and carriers with repeated exceptions. Archive the open-item rollforward, source exports, decisions, and reconciliation.
