## Define the coding rule
Write the account, class, department, project, and receipt requirements for common transactions. A Philippines bookkeeper can follow the rule consistently when the business provides examples and a named owner for exceptions.

## Preserve the business purpose
The merchant name alone rarely explains whether a cost is a client expense, supply, subscription, travel item, or owner-related transaction. Require a short purpose and source document where the policy calls for it.

## Create a review queue
Flag new vendors, unusual amounts, split charges, missing receipts, and transactions that cross departments. A queue gives the reviewer a manageable decision set and prevents assumptions from becoming permanent mappings.

## Do not turn coding into approval
The bookkeeper may record an approved transaction and identify missing approval. The budget owner or finance lead decides whether the spend was authorized and whether a policy exception is acceptable.

## Check changes over time
Compare recurring vendors and account use with prior periods. A sudden shift may reflect a real business change, a new system mapping, or a miscoding pattern. Investigate the reason before standardizing it.

## Hand off the exceptions
Send the reviewer a list with transaction, proposed treatment, evidence, question, and deadline. Record the decision in the accounting trail so future cycles become faster without losing judgment.
