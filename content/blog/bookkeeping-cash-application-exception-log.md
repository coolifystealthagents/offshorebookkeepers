## Start with the bank evidence

Capture the deposit or processor report, settlement date, payer name, reference supplied, possible matches, and amount before looking for a match. A source-first record prevents a later note from becoming the only evidence.

## Separate research from resolution

The bookkeeper can compare open invoices, customer correspondence, and prior payment patterns when that work is within the written scope. The log should distinguish a likely match from an approved match. Never clear an item merely because one invoice has a similar amount; record the reason and the reviewer’s decision.

## Close the loop with the ledger

After approval, link the applied transaction to the exception row and retain the original evidence. Review the remaining unapplied balance by age and owner. Repeated patterns may indicate an invoice-reference problem, a deposit batching issue, or a customer master-data gap that deserves a process decision.

## Review the queue on a rhythm

A short review of new, aging, and blocked exceptions keeps cash visibility from depending on one person’s memory. The output should show what is ready, what needs a decision, and what evidence is still missing.

## Make the handoff reviewable

Keep the final packet with its source links, status, reviewer, and next review date. A clear preparation boundary lets Offshore Bookkeepers support recurring finance work while the business keeps control of approvals and accounting decisions.
