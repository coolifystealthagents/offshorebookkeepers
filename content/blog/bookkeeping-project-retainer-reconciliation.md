## Define the review boundary

Start with the reporting date, accounts or projects included, and the person who approves exceptions. This project retainer reconciliation should explain its scope before anyone changes a record.

## Build the evidence packet

Collect contract, deposit, approved invoice, application, refund, and control account. Preserve the original source and give each unresolved item a named owner and due date. A bookkeeper can prepare the detail and document questions without making a policy or accounting judgment.

## Reconcile before presenting

Roll each client's retainer from cash receipt through approved applications, invoices, refunds, and ending available balance. Compare the client-level register with bank and liability accounts, and investigate negative retainers, cross-client use, or applications lacking billing support.

## Keep approval independent

The bookkeeper can update the retainer ledger and propose supported matches. Engagement and finance owners approve when funds may be applied or returned, how disputes are handled, and whether any balance requires reclassification or write-off.

## Close with a useful handoff

The final packet should show the period, source reports, reconciled totals, open exceptions, reviewer decision, and next review date. That structure makes project retainer reconciliation useful for owners and finance managers who need dependable books without losing control.
