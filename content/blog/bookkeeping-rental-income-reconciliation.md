## Define the review boundary

Start with the reporting date, accounts or projects included, and the person who approves exceptions. This rental income reconciliation should explain its scope before anyone changes a record.

## Build the evidence packet

Collect property, unit, rent roll, invoice, receipt, deposit, credit, and delinquency. Preserve the original source and give each unresolved item a named owner and due date. A bookkeeper can prepare the detail and document questions without making a policy or accounting judgment.

## Reconcile before presenting

Compare the rent roll and lease charges with tenant receipts, concessions, credits, security deposits, property-manager statements, and rental-income ledger by unit. Separate vacancy, delinquency, unapplied cash, and lease-change timing rather than netting them into one variance.

## Keep approval independent

A bookkeeper may post approved charges and prepare tenant-level exceptions. Property management confirms occupancy and lease facts, while the authorized finance owner approves concessions, deposit applications, bad-debt decisions, rent adjustments, and accounting treatment.

## Close with a useful handoff

The final packet should show the period, source reports, reconciled totals, open exceptions, reviewer decision, and next review date. That structure makes rental income reconciliation useful for owners and finance managers who need dependable books without losing control.
