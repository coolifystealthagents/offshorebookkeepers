The bookkeeper who runs the close is on leave. The replacement opens the folder, finds a trial balance and a list of account names, and spends two days reconstructing which items were accrued, which were paid, and who approved the odd journal entry.

A month-end close handoff note prevents that. It records the open items, the evidence, and the decisions while the close is still in progress.

## Write during the close

Add to the note as each item is handled, not at the end. The context is fresh and the evidence is still in the folder.

A note written after the close is a summary. A note written during the close is an instruction the next person can follow.

## List the open items

For each open item, record the account, the amount, the evidence, the owner, and the next action. Include the date the item was opened.

An item without an evidence link is a task, not a close item. The next person should be able to open the source from the note.

## Separate the judgment calls

Record the accruals, the estimates, and the reclassifications that required a decision. Add who approved each and the basis.

The judgment calls are the part most likely to be repeated differently. A short note on the basis prevents a new accountant from reversing a deliberate choice.

## Record the reconciliation status

For each reconciled account, record the balance, the date, and the person who signed off. For the unreconciled ones, record the difference and the plan.

A note that says "reconciled" without a date and a name is a claim. The reviewer needs the evidence trail.

## What the note can show

The note can show what was done, what remains, and who owns each item. It can show the judgments and the approvals behind the numbers.

That is enough for a new accountant to continue the close without restarting it.

## What the note cannot show

The note cannot show whether the numbers are correct or whether the accounting policy is appropriate. It records the process and the evidence.

It also cannot replace the source documents. The note points to them.

## Test the handoff

Once a quarter, ask someone who did not run the close to complete one open item using only the note. Record the questions they ask.

The questions are the gap list. Fix the note, not the person.

## Keep the note short

Use a table with the item, the amount, the evidence, the owner, the next action, and the due date. A long narrative will not be updated.

The note should take minutes to read and seconds to add to. That is what makes it survive a busy close.

## A worked example

A close has 14 open items. The note lists nine accruals, three reconciliations, and two approvals. The replacement accountant clears the accruals from the evidence links and escalates one approval.

The close finishes on time and the reviewer can see who approved each judgment. The note did the work that a verbal handover could not.

## What the note cannot do

The note cannot make a late close on time. It makes the work transferable and the evidence visible.

Used every month, it turns the close from a personal routine into a process the team can cover.
