## Scope and publication note

Published October 5, 2026. This article addresses bookkeeping preparation and review. It is not accounting, tax, legal, environmental, transportation, safety, or regulatory advice.

## A disposal invoice starts with a physical load

A waste hauler can collect several containers before a truck reaches a landfill or transfer station. The facility weighs the vehicle, classifies the material, applies its price schedule, and issues a scale ticket. Days later, accounts payable receives a statement that may group dozens of tickets. Customer invoices can include fixed service, excess-weight fees, contamination charges, or a disposal surcharge under different contract rules. A monthly vendor total cannot show whether those customer charges came from the correct loads.

The reconciliation should begin with a load key. Combine the route or work-order ID, truck, driver or crew identifier allowed by policy, facility, inbound time, outbound time, ticket number, and service date. Add gross, tare, and net weight; unit of measure; material class; facility rate; taxes or regulated fees; vendor charge; customer stops; and billing status. Preserve the scale ticket image or authorized electronic record rather than transcribing only its total.

## Establish which source owns each fact

Dispatch owns the planned route, but it may not prove that a container was serviced. Telematics can support location and time, but it may not identify the material. The scale system supplies measured weight and facility classification. The signed customer agreement or approved rate table supplies the billing rule. The vendor invoice supplies the amount requested for payment. Write this source hierarchy into the procedure so a preparer does not resolve conflicts by choosing whichever total is easiest to post.

The EPA provides [basic information about landfills](https://www.epa.gov/landfills/basic-information-about-landfills), including the distinct purposes of municipal solid-waste, industrial-waste, and hazardous-waste facilities. That background explains why material and facility classifications matter, but it does not determine a hauler's accounting or customer price. The [IRS recordkeeping guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping) provides general support for retaining business transaction evidence. Authorized environmental, operations, legal, and finance owners define the company's specific rules.

## Rebuild the facility charge from the ticket

For each ticket, reperform net weight from gross less tare when the source provides both values. Convert pounds, tons, kilograms, or cubic measures only under the approved conversion table. Then apply the facility rate that was effective on the service date, keeping minimum charges, special material fees, taxes, fuel components, and manual adjustments on separate lines. Compare the calculated amount with the ticket and later vendor invoice.

Do not overwrite a facility classification because the dispatch description differs. Record the mismatch and link both sources. A load scheduled as construction debris but ticketed as mixed municipal waste may carry a different rate and require an operational review. The bookkeeper identifies the dollar effect and asks a focused question; the qualified owner determines whether the ticket, route coding, or customer record should change.

## Allocate a mixed route without hiding assumptions

Suppose Route R-204 services three roll-off customers and reaches the facility at a net 8.4 tons. Customer A's container has an approved on-board weight of 3.1 tons, Customer B's has 2.6 tons, and Customer C's has no usable weight because its lift sensor failed. The route also records 0.4 tons of yard cleanup loaded separately. The workpaper does not give Customer C the unexplained remainder and call the route complete.

First preserve the 8.4-ton facility control total. Then show 3.1 and 2.6 tons as measured customer quantities, 0.4 tons as company cleanup, and 2.3 tons as an unresolved customer allocation. If the approved fallback method uses container volume, historical density, or a documented manual estimate, calculate it in a separate column, label the method, and obtain the required approval. The original missing measurement remains visible even after a billing allocation is authorized.

## Keep vendor cost and customer billing in parallel

The facility may charge $92 per ton while customer agreements use a fixed allowance, actual weight, a published disposal index, or a separately approved surcharge. Build one side of the schedule for vendor cost and another for customer billing. Link them through the load and customer allocation, but do not assume that vendor price automatically becomes the customer rate.

Track allowance quantity, billable excess, contract rate version, billed amount, credit, dispute, and final status by customer. If a facility later credits a duplicate ticket, connect the credit to the original load. Then determine whether a customer credit is needed through the authorized commercial process. Bookkeeping supplies the evidence and arithmetic; it does not revise contract terms or promise a credit.

## Treat rejected and duplicate tickets as cases

Useful exception states include ticket missing, unreadable ticket, duplicate number, gross-tare error, unit mismatch, vehicle mismatch, facility-class mismatch, rate mismatch, invoice omitted, invoice duplicated, allocation unsupported, customer rule unclear, credit pending, and disputed. Each case needs the observed conflict, amount, evidence links, owner, requested action, due date, and age.

Watch for the same ticket appearing on two facility statements, a reversed ticket followed by a corrected ticket, and ticket numbers reused across facilities. A stable key should include facility identity rather than ticket number alone. When a correction arrives, retain the original and reversal so the monthly bridge explains both vendor and ledger movements.

## Respect operational and payment boundaries

An offshore bookkeeper can collect approved exports, index tickets, compare identifiers, reperform weights and rates, maintain exceptions, and draft vendor or customer adjustments for review. The role should not classify regulated material, approve a disposal facility, alter a customer contract, release a vendor payment, issue a customer credit, or decide responsibility for contamination. Named operations, environmental, commercial, and finance owners retain those decisions.

Limit access to the information needed for reconciliation. Route records can reveal customer locations, service patterns, and driver activity. Use named accounts, approved storage, and documented retention. Separate vendor-master changes, invoice preparation, payment approval, customer-rate maintenance, and credit approval where practical.

## Close quantities before closing dollars

At month-end, account for every facility ticket in the route population and every vendor-invoice ticket in the ticket register. Reconcile ticket quantities by facility and material class before reconciling charges to accounts payable. Then tie approved customer allocations to invoice lines and receivables. Review routes with service but no disposal record, tickets with no route, negative weights, zero-weight customer charges, and post-cutoff credits.

Archive the route snapshot, scale tickets, facility rate versions, vendor statement bridge, allocation workpaper, customer billing comparison, exception decisions, and ledger tie-out. Report unallocated weight, unbilled approved surcharges, duplicate-ticket exposure, pending credits, and oldest open case as workflow indicators rather than performance claims.
