## Define the review boundary

Start with the reporting date, accounts or projects included, and the person who approves exceptions. This credit card statement reconciliation should explain its scope before anyone changes a record.

## Build the evidence packet

Collect statement balance, merchant, date, amount, receipt, and business purpose. Preserve the original source and give each unresolved item a named owner and due date. A bookkeeper can prepare the detail and document questions without making a policy or accounting judgment.

## Reconcile before presenting

Reconcile each issuer statement from prior balance through purchases, credits, fees, and payments to the card ledger. List missing receipts, duplicate imports, personal-charge candidates, and statement transactions absent from the books without plugging the difference to expense.

## Keep approval independent

The preparer may collect receipts, suggest coding, and assemble the tie-out. Cardholders or managers substantiate business purpose, and the finance approver decides personal-charge recovery, policy exceptions, write-offs, and final account classification independently of the preparer.

## Close with a useful handoff

The final packet should show the period, source reports, reconciled totals, open exceptions, reviewer decision, and next review date. That structure makes credit card statement reconciliation useful for owners and finance managers who need dependable books without losing control.
