## Set the decision boundary

Start cash receipts reconciliation by writing what the support role prepares and what the business decides. The bookkeeper can gather records, follow the documented process, update the accounting system, and explain an exception. The owner, controller, or CPA should retain approval, payment release, accounting judgment, and policy exceptions.

## Define the recurring input

List the source records, expected timing, format, account or period reference, and the evidence needed for cash receipts reconciliation. If an item arrives through an unusual channel, record it and move it to a hold queue. Clear intake rules expose missing information before it becomes rework.

## Use a repeatable sequence

Break cash receipts reconciliation into receive, check, record, compare, document, and hand off. Each step needs one expected output and one stop condition. Do not silently resolve an unfamiliar exception. Record what was checked, what is missing, and which named owner must decide.

## Keep evidence with the work

Tie each receipt from bank or processor activity to its remittance, customer, invoice allocation, fee or deduction, deposit batch, and ledger entry. Set retention expectations under the client's policy, using [IRS recordkeeping guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping) as a general reference.

## Separate preparation from approval

Give the bookkeeper enough access to complete cash receipts reconciliation without giving the same person authority to approve their own work. Define the reviewer, deadline, review questions, and close event. Use named accounts and multifactor authentication for system access.

## Create an exception path

Queue unidentified deposits, short pays, overpayments, chargebacks, and receipts applied to closed invoices by reason. The preparer documents candidate matches; the receivables owner approves credits, refunds, write-offs, or changes to customer allocation.

## Review representative cycles

Validate the workflow on ACH remittances, check deposits, card settlements, and one receipt covering multiple invoices. Review whether gross receipts, processor deductions, and posting dates remain traceable before scaling daily application volume.

## Make the handoff explicit

Hand off a deposit-to-ledger control total plus unapplied receipts, disputed deductions, reversals, and customer questions with named owners.
