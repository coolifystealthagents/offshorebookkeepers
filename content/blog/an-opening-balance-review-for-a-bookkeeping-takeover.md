## Start with the handover, not the import

A bookkeeping takeover starts with a file that already has a history. The new team is expected to continue from balances someone else prepared, often with limited explanation. The opening trial balance may come from a prior bookkeeper, an owner, or a system migration. Each account carries assumptions that are rarely written down, and those assumptions become the new team's problem on the first close.

An opening balance review is not a full audit and it is not a rewrite of prior work. It is a bounded check that asks whether the starting position is supported well enough to build on. The review identifies which balances are agreed, which are provisional, and which need an owner decision. It also creates the evidence index that the team will use when a later question comes back to the opening period.

## Why the opening trial balance needs a review

An old balance is not automatically wrong, but it is a problem when nobody can show the document, calculation, or approval behind it. A bank balance can be confirmed to a statement, while an accrued liability or a fixed-asset total may depend on a schedule that no longer exists. The review separates those cases so that a few clean balances do not hide the unsupported ones. It also gives the new team a defensible reason for every opening number it keeps.

The review protects both sides of the handover. The prior team gets a record of what was delivered, and the new team gets a limit on what it inherited. Without that record, every later correction becomes an argument about whose period the error belongs to. A short written review is cheaper than reconstructing the same history during a dispute or a tax notice.

## Agree the cutover date and source set

The cutover date is the line between the prior team's work and the new team's responsibility. Write it down with the time zone and the last transaction included. If the prior team closed a month, the opening balance should agree to that close, not to a later bank balance that includes new activity. A written cutover prevents two people from posting the same transaction in different periods.

List the reports that define the opening position: trial balance, general ledger detail, aged payables, aged receivables, fixed-asset register, debt schedule, and payroll liability report. Record the report date and the system that produced each one. If a report is unavailable, note the substitute and the reason. The source set becomes the evidence index for every later question about the opening position.

## Tie the trial balance to the last filed return

The last filed tax return or financial statement is an independent reference for the opening position. Compare the trial balance to the return at the account or grouping level. Do not expect every line to match, because timing and presentation can differ. Investigate differences that affect equity, tax liability, or a balance the owner relies on for a lending or partner decision.

Prepare a bridge for each material difference and label it as timing, presentation, or an unreconciled gap. A prior-year depreciation difference may be a schedule issue, while a payroll tax difference may indicate an unpaid liability. The bridge should be short enough to review but specific enough to support a decision. Send unresolved tax questions to the tax preparer rather than guessing in the ledger.

## Test the accounts that carry judgment

Cash and credit card balances are the easiest to confirm because a statement exists. Start there, but do not stop at the total. Reconcile each bank account to its statement and check that the ledger includes uncleared items and any restriction on the cash. A balance that agrees in total can still contain a duplicate transfer or an item that belongs to another entity.

Accruals, prepaids, inventory, fixed assets, and revenue cutoffs need a different test. Ask for the schedule behind each balance and tie a sample of items to invoices, contracts, or calculations. Check that the schedule covers the same period as the trial balance. If the schedule is missing, record the account as unsupported and propose a review step before the first close. The [fixed-asset register handoff](/blog/bookkeeping-fixed-asset-register-handoff) shows how one schedule can be rebuilt without overwriting history.

## Put an owner and a next action on each exception

An exception list without owners becomes a list of complaints. For each item, record the account, the amount, the evidence received, the fact still missing, the person who can supply it, and the date it entered the list. Use the same fields for every row so the list can be sorted by age or owner. A reviewer should be able to read one row and know what happens next.

Decide which items block the first close and which can be carried with a documented limitation. A small immaterial difference may be accepted by the owner, while a payroll liability or a bank restriction should not be. Record the decision and the date it was made. Do not let the new team silently absorb the prior team's unresolved work, because that hides both the cost and the risk.

## Hand over with a named reviewer

The opening review ends with a short memo covering scope, cutover date, sources reviewed, balances agreed, exceptions accepted, and items still open. Attach the evidence index and the exception list. The memo should be signed by the preparer and acknowledged by the finance owner. This is the record that the new team relied on when it began posting. Keep the memo short enough to read before the first transaction is posted.

The first close is the real test of the opening position. Compare the first set of reconciliations to the opening balances and check that every prior exception is either resolved or still visible. If a new difference appears, trace it to the opening review before changing a prior entry. A clean handover does not guarantee a clean first close, but it makes the cause of a new problem much easier to find.

The opening review also sets the tone for the working relationship. A team that documents its questions and limits earns more trust than one that promises a perfect cleanup in the first week. Start with the accounts that affect cash, tax, and reported equity, then expand the sample as the evidence improves.
