August 20, 2026 operating guide. This article discusses bookkeeping support around payroll records and is not payroll, tax, employment, or legal advice. Local rules and client authorization control the process.

## Name the source of truth
Begin with the payroll register or provider report that the client has approved for the period. Capture provider, entity, pay date, covered dates, currency, version, retrieval time, and approver. The offshore bookkeeper should not infer payroll from a bank withdrawal alone. A bank amount can confirm movement of cash but cannot by itself explain gross pay, deductions, employer costs, or liabilities. Keep the source immutable and work from a copy or linked record.

## Separate changes from routine files
New hires, terminations, pay changes, bonuses, leave, deductions, and corrections need a visible change route. The bookkeeper may check whether an approved change appears in the register and flag a mismatch. The role should not approve its own payroll change or rely on an informal chat instruction. Store the change request, effective date, approver, and provider result. If the register differs, stop the journal preparation until the owner answers.

## Build a source checklist
List the register, funding report, payroll liability detail, journal mapping, prior-period comparison, approved exception list, and any client-specific reconciliation evidence. Mark each received, checked, missing, or not applicable. A complete packet is not just a folder with files. It has a clear period, entity, version, owner, and conclusion. The reviewer should know which document supports each amount in the draft journal.

## Keep employee data limited
Payroll records contain sensitive information. Use the client-approved system and role-based access, and expose only fields needed for the assigned bookkeeping task. Avoid moving full employee records into broad spreadsheets or messages. Named accounts and multifactor authentication are basic operating requirements. When access changes, review and remove it promptly. The offshore role needs enough evidence to reconcile payroll, not unrestricted access to every personnel record.

## Prepare the journal bridge
Map the register totals to the journal lines the client has approved: expense categories, liabilities, employer costs, and cash or clearing accounts. Show source total, mapped total, difference, and explanation. Never plug a difference to force the bridge to balance. The bookkeeper can identify a mapping issue or missing line, but an owner decides whether a new account, correction, or policy treatment is appropriate.

## Reconcile funding separately
Compare the approved funding amount to the bank movement and payment confirmation. A timing difference should have a documented explanation and expected resolution date. Do not treat a bank withdrawal as proof that the payroll register was correct. Likewise, do not treat a matching register as proof that the funding settled. The separate checks help the reviewer identify whether the issue is payroll preparation, cash timing, or ledger posting.

## Treat liabilities as open until supported
Payroll taxes, benefits, deductions, and other liabilities may follow different payment dates and owners. Record the amount, source, due or expected date, payment status, and evidence location. The bookkeeper can maintain the schedule and reconcile known payments. The authorized payroll or finance owner decides on filings, exceptions, corrections, and any communication with employees or agencies. Avoid promising that a liability is settled when only the journal has posted.

## Review period changes
Compare current and prior register totals by useful categories while respecting the client’s access and reporting policy. Investigate unexpected movements, new categories, missing employees, duplicate lines, and changes in employer cost. A variance is a question, not proof of error. Document the source and explanation supplied by the owner. Keep the comparison with the packet so a later reviewer can see how the question was resolved.

## Define close deadlines
Set deadlines for source receipt, first check, exception route, journal draft, reviewer response, posting, and reconciliation. Include a time-zone convention and a fallback owner for holidays or provider delays. The offshore bookkeeper can update the calendar, request missing files, and prepare status summaries. The finance owner controls period locks, late adjustments, and final close acceptance. A deadline without a named decision owner only hides delay.

## Test access and handoffs
Use a normal payroll, a late correction, a missing register, a funding mismatch, and an employee change that appears after the cutoff. Ask another preparer to follow the packet checklist. Test who receives a question when the client-side reviewer is unavailable. Record where the handoff failed and repair the procedure. Do not expand access simply because a deadline is approaching.

## Retain a reviewer decision
The packet should end with reviewer name, date, scope checked, exceptions accepted or returned, journal status, funding status, and open liability items. Preserve the approval record according to client policy. The bookkeeper can prepare the summary and update the tracker. The authorized reviewer owns the conclusion. If the conclusion changes later, retain the new decision and link it to the original packet.

## Practical conclusion

The closing check compares the approved payroll summary, funding or payment evidence, payroll clearing activity, and the journal packet. Record the period, entity, currency, source version, preparer, reviewer, and open questions. A bookkeeper can identify a mismatch and prepare the bridge, but should not change payroll inputs or decide how an unexplained difference affects an employee. Route those questions to the payroll or finance owner with the exact source lines that conflict.

Keep a cut-off log for late changes. It should show when an item arrived, whether it entered the current cycle, who approved the treatment, and where the updated source is stored. Retain the superseded packet so a reviewer can understand the sequence. This makes offshore bookkeeping support dependable across time zones without expanding the role into payroll administration or employment advice.
A payroll source pack makes offshore bookkeeping safer by fixing the approved register, separating change requests, limiting employee access, bridging journal lines, reconciling funding, tracking liabilities, and assigning deadlines. The support role prepares evidence and raises mismatches. Authorized payroll and finance owners retain approval, policy interpretation, employee decisions, filings, period locks, and final review. That boundary lets routine records move efficiently without confusing a bookkeeping handoff with payroll authority.
