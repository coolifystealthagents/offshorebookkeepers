## Define the request record
Require a dated request with a stable identifier that names the vendor, entity, requested change, business reason, requester, supporting documents, effective date, and approver. Preserve the original details beside later corrections so the reviewer can reconstruct the change and distinguish a genuine update from a message that only appears familiar.

## Separate a new vendor from a changed vendor
For a bank-detail change, preserve the requester, vendor ID, old and new details, effective date, independent verification, and approver. A Philippines bookkeeper can compare the request with existing records, flag a mismatch, and prepare the update; they should not treat an email reply as sufficient verification or release a payment based on it.

## Protect the bank field
Treat bank details as a high-risk change. The bookkeeper can log the request and compare it with existing records, but an authorized owner should verify it using a trusted phone number or contact path already held by the business.

## Check duplicates
Search names, addresses, tax identifiers, and bank details for duplicates before creating a record. A duplicate vendor can split history, create duplicate payments, or make later review difficult.

## Separate roles
The person who maintains a vendor record should not independently approve an invoice or release a payment. Use system permissions and a written process to preserve that separation.

## Review the master and change log
Sample new vendors, changed bank details, duplicate tax identifiers, renamed vendors, inactive records, and unusual payment patterns. Check the old value, new value, request, evidence, approver, timestamp, and related invoice activity. If the master record conflicts with a bill, stop the transaction and route the discrepancy; editing the master to make a payment pass would remove evidence the reviewer needs.

## Escalate suspicious requests
Pause the record, preserve the message and metadata, and alert the finance owner through a known channel. Speed is not a reason to weaken verification for an offshore bookkeeping handoff.
