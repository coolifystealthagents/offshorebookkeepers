## Define the recharge basis
Record the client-approved agreement, participating entities, covered period, service or cost being recharged, source costs, allocation key, approved markup rule, currency, and named approver before calculating a recharge. If the basis is missing or conflicts with the source records, hold the line as an exception rather than choosing a method.

## Build the schedule from evidence
Link source invoices, payroll or vendor costs, time records, and the approved allocation. Record assumptions separately so the reviewer can challenge them without rebuilding the entire calculation.

## Show the allocation in a bridge
If Entity A paid a shared software invoice, the workpaper should show the source bill, service period, allocation driver, each entity’s share, any currency conversion, and the proposed intercompany entry. A Philippines bookkeeper can calculate the documented split and flag an unsupported markup. The entity owners approve the basis and treatment.

## Post both sides deliberately
Use matching references, dates, currencies, and counterparty names. A support role can prepare or post under a documented rule, but an entity owner should approve a new basis or unusual balance.

## Match and confirm both sides
Compare the sender’s recharge receivable with the recipient’s payable, including the invoice number, amount, currency, and period, and request confirmation from the other entity. A difference may arise from timing, an exchange-rate choice, or a missing entry; do not force the accounts to agree by overwriting one side. Preserve correspondence, confirmations, and unresolved differences in the shared reconciliation file, and route policy questions to the authorized finance reviewer.

## Age the exceptions
Sort differences by period and cause: timing, missing invoice, currency, duplicate, coding, or disputed allocation. Escalate old items and policy questions instead of rolling them forward without explanation.

## Prepare the consolidation handoff
The final pack should show the schedule, entries, confirmations, open items, and reviewer decision. That gives a controller the evidence needed for close while preserving entity-level accountability.
