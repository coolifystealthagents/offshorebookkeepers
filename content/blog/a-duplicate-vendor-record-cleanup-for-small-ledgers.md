A vendor is entered as "Northside Cleaning" in January and "Northside Cleaning Co" in March. Both have open bills, the payments are split, and the year-end 1099 report lists two vendors with the same tax identifier. The cleanup takes a day and could have been avoided by a search.

A duplicate vendor cleanup is a short routine that finds the records and merges them safely. It protects the payment history, the tax reporting, and the vendor relationship.

## Search before you create

Before adding a vendor, search by name, tax identifier, and bank details. A partial name, a different case, or a punctuation difference can hide the existing record.

Record the search performed. A note that says "checked" is enough; the point is that the check happened.

## Find the candidates

Run a report of vendors sorted by name and scan for near matches. Then check the tax identifier and the bank account for exact duplicates.

Record the candidate pairs and the evidence. A pair with the same name but different identifiers is not a duplicate.

## Check the open items

Before merging, list the open bills, payments, and credits for both records. A merge that ignores an open bill can lose the liability.

Record the items and their new owner after the merge. The audit trail should show where each item went.

## Merge with care

Use the system's merge function where it exists. If it does not, rename the duplicate and mark it inactive rather than deleting it.

Record the merge date and the surviving identifier. Keep the old identifier in a note for reference.

## Check the tax reporting

Confirm that the merged vendor produces one report and one total. A duplicate that survives into the tax file is the most expensive kind.

Record the check and the correction. The vendor should receive one form, not two.

## What the cleanup can show

The cleanup can show that the vendor list was reviewed and the duplicates resolved. It can show the payments and the tax reporting now agree.

It can support a cleaner accounts payable process. A vendor list with one record per supplier is easier to reconcile and audit.

## What the cleanup cannot show

The cleanup cannot show that the underlying bills were correct. A merged record with a wrong invoice is still wrong.

It cannot show that the vendor is legitimate. That needs the onboarding checks.

It also cannot prevent new duplicates. Only the search habit and the quarterly review do that.

## A worked example

A ledger has two records for the same cleaner. The open bills are moved to the surviving record, the duplicate is marked inactive, and the tax report shows one vendor.

The cleanup takes twenty minutes and prevents a second 1099. The note records the merge and the identifier.

## Communicate the merge

Tell the vendor which record survives and confirm the remittance details. A vendor who receives two statements or two forms will call.

Record the confirmation. A short email is enough, and it prevents a duplicate payment to the inactive record.

## What to do with a duplicate payment

If a payment was made to both records, record the duplicate and request a refund or apply it to the next bill. Do not delete the payment history.

Record the resolution and the date. The vendor statement should show one balance after the correction.

## A worked example

A ledger has three records for the same supplier. Two have open bills, and one has no activity. The bills are moved, the inactive record is marked, and the tax report shows one vendor.

The cleanup takes fifteen minutes and prevents a duplicate form. The note records the merge and the identifier.

## Review the vendor list quarterly

Scan the list for new near matches and check the tax identifiers. A quarterly review is faster than an annual cleanup.

Record the findings and the merges. The list should get shorter, not longer.

## Conclusion

A duplicate vendor cleanup protects the payment history and the tax reporting. It shows the duplicates, the open items, and the merge. It does not fix a wrong invoice or a bad vendor, and it should not be treated as a substitute for onboarding.
