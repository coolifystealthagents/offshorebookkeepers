## Treat the disposal notice as an intake event

Operations may describe an asset as sold, scrapped, donated, transferred, missing, or simply no longer used. Those statements do not mean the same thing. Open a disposal case and record who reported the event, the stated event date, location, asset tag, description, and proposed outcome. Ask for the source document required by company policy, such as an approved disposal form, bill of sale, pickup record, insurance report, or transfer receipt. An offshore bookkeeper can organize this intake. The asset custodian confirms the physical event, and the controller or CPA decides accounting and tax treatment. Keep the register unchanged while identity or authorization remains uncertain.

## Prove which register record is involved

Match the asset tag, serial number, location, custodian, acquisition document, and register description. Similar laptops, vehicles, or machines are easy to confuse when descriptions are generic. If the physical tag is unreadable, document the alternative evidence and send the ambiguity to the reviewer. Pull original cost, acquisition date, placed-in-service date, accumulated depreciation, prior transfers, and component links from the controlled register. Do not overwrite these historical fields in a working copy. The [IRS recordkeeping publication](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p583) describes keeping records for assets and business transactions; company policy and professional advice determine the specific retention and treatment required.

## Build a disposal packet around facts

Use a cover sheet with case ID, entity, asset ID, event type, event date, approval status, proceeds status, and preparer. Behind it, link the original purchase evidence, register record, custodian confirmation, disposal authorization, sale or removal evidence, cash receipt, related costs, and proposed register change. Separate documents received from calculations prepared by bookkeeping. If someone supplies an email after the event, retain its actual date rather than presenting it as contemporaneous approval. [Bookkeeping services](/services/month-end-close-support) can maintain the packet and chase routine missing items, while management owns exceptions and final authorization.

## Reconcile proceeds without assuming the answer

For a sale, compare the buyer, asset description, gross price, taxes or fees, payment date, payment method, and net receipt. Trace the receipt to the bank deposit and customer or miscellaneous receivable if one was used. Record selling costs separately. If multiple assets were sold for one price, do not invent an allocation; ask the policy owner for an approved basis. A trade-in also needs separate visibility for the old asset, new asset, cash paid, and dealer documentation. For scrap, donation, loss, or abandonment, state that no proceeds were identified and show the evidence supporting that fact.

## Prepare calculations for review, not conclusions

The workpaper can show register cost, accumulated depreciation through the last posted period, net book value per the register, gross proceeds, related costs, and the arithmetic difference. Label every value by source. Do not decide the disposal date, final depreciation, impairment, gain or loss classification, or tax result unless the approved procedure explicitly provides the inputs and an authorized reviewer signs off. Where policy is unclear, leave a bounded question. Formula cells should be locked or reviewed, and manual overrides should include a reason. This division lets remote support do useful preparation without drifting into protected judgment.

## Update systems in an ordered sequence

After approval, post the authorized journal through the normal entry workflow, capture its ID, and update the fixed-asset register with the disposal date and case reference. Do not delete the asset row. Mark it disposed so historical reporting remains traceable. Update any asset subledger, insurance list, property schedule, or operational inventory named in the procedure, but avoid expanding access just to complete a one-off task. If systems update on different dates, track each completion separately. The [fixed-asset register handoff](/blog/bookkeeping-fixed-asset-register-handoff) helps define fields and ownership before this work is delegated.

## Reconcile the close and exception list

At month end, tie disposal journals to register changes and case packets. Compare sale proceeds in the workpaper with bank receipts and any receivable clearing. Review assets marked pending disposal, missing, transferred, or awaiting approval. An operating event before cutoff with late paperwork should remain visible for controller review; the bookkeeper should not choose a period to make the list disappear. [Management reporting support](/services/management-reporting-support) can surface material pending cases and unusual proceeds. Reconcile the ending register to the general ledger after additions, depreciation, transfers, and disposals.

## Use retrospective checks to improve intake

Sample closed cases for exact asset identification, prior approval, complete evidence, proceeds tie-out, journal approval, and retained history. Look for patterns: assets removed from operations months before finance was notified, repeated missing tags, proceeds deposited without a case ID, or register rows deleted instead of closed. Assign corrective work to the process owner rather than silently cleaning the spreadsheet. Access reviews should also confirm that preparers cannot approve their own journals or change bank data. A dependable disposal process makes the awkward cases visible and preserves enough history for someone new to understand what happened.

## Pilot the handoff with two different outcomes

Choose one straightforward sale and one noncash event such as a transfer or scrapping. The sale tests whether the team can connect the asset identity, approval, buyer document, bank receipt, costs, calculation, journal, and register status. The noncash event tests whether the procedure works when there is no deposit to anchor the review. Ask a reviewer who did not prepare the cases to reconstruct both histories. Note every point where that person needs a private message, unexplained spreadsheet color, or undocumented system search. Fix those gaps before adding more locations or asset classes. Review permissions at the same time: the preparer needs enough access to gather records and propose updates, but not authority to approve disposal, release money, or erase register history. Repeat the test when systems, custodians, or policy owners change.
