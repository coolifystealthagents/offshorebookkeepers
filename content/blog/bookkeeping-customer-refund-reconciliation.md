## Define the refund record
Capture customer, original transaction, reason, amount, channel, approval, refund date, and ledger reference. Give each case a stable ID.
## Verify the source trail
Compare the request to the original receipt, order, credit memo, and payment record. The [IRS recordkeeping guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping) supports retaining documents that substantiate business transactions.
## Route the decision
The bookkeeper can check the packet, prepare the entry, and reconcile the result. A named owner decides policy exceptions, large refunds, and disputed claims.
## Tie it to existing controls
Use the [cash application guide](/blog/cash-application-bookkeeping-support) and [bank reconciliation checklist](/blog/bank-reconciliation-support-checklist) to find refunds that cleared but lack complete support. Log unresolved differences until closed.
## Review the queue
Inspect duplicate requests, old credits, unusual destinations, and repeated reasons. Review two cycles before adding more systems or refund types.
