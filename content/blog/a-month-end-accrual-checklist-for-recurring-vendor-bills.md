A cleaning contract is billed on the fifth of the following month. The service for June is delivered in June, but the invoice arrives in July and is recorded there. June looks cheaper than it was, and July looks more expensive.

A recurring-bill accrual checklist keeps the expense in the right period. It is a short list of vendors, the service period, and the estimated amount, reviewed before the close.

## List the recurring vendors

Start with the vendors that bill on a cycle: cleaning, security, software, rent, utilities, and professional services. Record the cycle and the usual invoice date.

A vendor that bills irregularly may still need an accrual. Add it when the service period crosses the month boundary, even if the invoice is unpredictable.

## Accrue the service, not the invoice

The accrual is for the service received, not the invoice expected. Estimate from the contract, the prior invoice, or the days used, and record the basis.

Do not wait for a perfect number. A documented estimate that is corrected when the invoice arrives is better than an expense recorded in the wrong month.

## Record the reversal

Every accrual needs a reversal date and a method. Common practice is to reverse at the start of the next period and record the actual invoice when it arrives.

Record both dates in the checklist. An accrual without a reversal is a duplicate waiting to happen, and the next month's close will not catch it.

## Check the service period

Confirm the period the service covers. A quarterly bill may cover three months, and recording the full amount in one period distorts the trend.

Allocate by month where the contract allows. Record the allocation and keep it consistent across periods.

## Review the prior accruals

Before adding new accruals, check the ones from last month. An accrual that was not reversed is a liability that will double when the invoice arrives.

Clear the prior list as part of the checklist. The review takes minutes and prevents the most common accrual error.

## Reconcile when the invoice arrives

Compare the invoice to the accrual and record the difference. A small difference is normal; a large one may indicate a change in the contract or a missed service.

Record the explanation. The note makes the next estimate better and shows the reviewer that the accrual was tested.

## Keep the checklist short

Limit the list to the vendors that matter and the amounts that would distort the close. A checklist with fifty lines will not be reviewed.

Review the list quarterly and remove the vendors that no longer bill on a cycle. The checklist should reflect the current business, not the history.

## Utility bills

Utility bills often arrive after the period they cover. Accrue the estimated usage for the month and reconcile when the bill arrives.

Record the meter read or the estimate basis. A utility accrual without a basis is a guess, and a guess is hard to defend at review.

## Rent and software

Rent and software subscriptions usually bill on a fixed cycle. Accrue the month that has been used even when the invoice is dated the first of the next month.

Check the contract for prepayment terms. A prepaid annual subscription belongs in the period it covers, not the month it was paid.

## A worked example

A cleaning vendor bills on the fifth for the prior month. At the June close the June service has been delivered but the invoice has not arrived.

The bookkeeper accrues the contracted monthly amount, records the basis, and sets a reversal for July. When the invoice arrives, it is matched to the accrual and the difference is recorded. June and July both show the right expense.

## Month-end timing

Set a cut-off for adding accruals and communicate it to the team. A late accrual added after the close distorts the comparison and confuses the report.

Record the cut-off in the checklist. The date should be early enough to review the list before the final close.

## Communication with the client

Tell the client which accruals were made and why. A short note prevents a question later and gives the client a chance to correct an estimate.

Where an amount is material, ask the client to confirm. The confirmation is part of the record and supports the estimate.

## What to review quarterly

Review the accrual list for vendors that no longer need an estimate. A vendor that moved to prepayment or changed its cycle should leave the list.

Record the change and the date. A stale checklist slowly becomes a list of historical habits rather than current obligations.

## What the checklist does not do

The checklist does not replace the client's accounting policy or the accountant's judgement about materiality. It applies the policy consistently.

It also does not prove the expense is correct. It shows that the expense was considered and recorded in the right period, which is the part the close controls.
