## Start with the decisions
Ask the report owner which decisions the pack is meant to support, which period and entities are in scope, and which definitions are approved. List the questions to answer, such as cash movement, overdue receivables, close status, project cost, or unusual expenses, and select reports that address them rather than collecting every available metric.

## Define each metric
Write the numerator, denominator, period, exclusions, source, and owner for every KPI. A Philippines bookkeeper can calculate a metric consistently when its definition does not change from month to month.

## Turn a metric into a question
If overdue receivables rose while cash receipts fell, the pack should show the aging movement, unapplied cash, disputed invoices, and owner for each significant item. If project cost increased, show the project key, approved budget or baseline if available, posted cost, and explanation. The bookkeeper assembles the evidence; management decides whether to change collection, scope, or spending.

## Make the pack traceable
Every reported number should link to a ledger report, reconciliation, or schedule with a period and extraction date. Reconcile headline totals to the trial balance or named operational source. If a metric uses a separate system, identify the timing difference rather than presenting the figures as interchangeable. Show exceptions separately from confirmed totals and label estimates.

## Write a variance note
A useful note says what changed, by how much, the likely cause, the supporting evidence, the owner, and the next action. Avoid attributing a result to a cause that has not been checked.

## Keep judgment with management
The support role prepares the pack and surfaces questions. Managers select the measures, interpret exceptions, and decide whether to change staffing, collections, purchasing, scope, or operating priorities.

## Archive the reviewed version
Save the final pack, source reports, calculations, and reviewer comments together. A versioned archive lets the next cycle explain changes without rewriting history.
