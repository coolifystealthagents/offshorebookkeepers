## Start with the decisions
List the questions the owner or manager needs answered: cash movement, overdue receivables, close status, project cost, or unusual expense. Select reports that answer those questions rather than collecting every available metric.

## Define each metric
Write numerator, denominator, period, exclusions, source, and owner for every KPI. A Philippines bookkeeper can calculate the number consistently when the definition does not change from month to month.

## Tie to the ledger
Reconcile headline totals to the trial balance or operational source and show the date of extraction. If a metric uses a separate system, name the timing difference and do not present the figures as interchangeable.

## Write a variance note
A useful note says what changed, by how much, likely cause, evidence, owner, and next action. Avoid attributing a result to a cause that has not been checked.

## Keep judgment with management
The support role prepares the pack and surfaces questions. Managers decide whether to change staffing, collections, purchasing, scope, or operating priorities.

## Archive the reviewed version
Save the final pack, source reports, calculations, and reviewer comments together. A versioned archive lets the next cycle explain changes without rewriting history.
