## Classify the entry first
Label entries as recurring approved, correcting, accrual, reclass, estimate, or policy-sensitive. The label tells the reviewer how much evidence and judgment is required and keeps a support role from treating every journal alike.

## Build the entry packet
Include the debit and credit accounts, amount, period, calculation, source records, explanation, and reversal rule if relevant. The Philippines bookkeeper can assemble this packet and identify missing assumptions before posting.

## Use a proposed state
Where access allows, keep a proposed journal separate from the posted ledger until review. If the system has no draft function, use a controlled approval log and restrict posting rights according to the written policy.

## Escalate judgment
An entry that changes revenue timing, reserves, tax treatment, impairment, owner activity, or a prior-period result needs a named decision maker. The bookkeeper supplies facts and options; the finance owner records the choice.

## Test recurring entries
Review recurring journals for changed amounts, expired schedules, duplicate postings, and source documents that no longer support the formula. A copied description is not proof that the entry remains valid.

## Close with evidence
Keep approved entries, rejected proposals, corrections, and reviewer notes together. This gives the next close a reliable starting point and makes the role boundary visible to everyone involved.
