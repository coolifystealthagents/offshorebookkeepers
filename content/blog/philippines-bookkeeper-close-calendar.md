## Map the close from outputs backward
Start with the reports and decisions the business needs, then list the reconciliations, schedules, source documents, and review steps that support them. A Philippines bookkeeping role is easier to manage when the calendar describes work rather than merely naming accounts.

## Give each task a complete definition
For every row, record the account or process, source expected, preparer, reviewer, due date, evidence link, and status. Add a stop condition such as “missing bank statement” or “policy decision required.” This prevents a bookkeeper from marking a task complete just because an entry was posted.

## Separate preparation from approval
The support role can gather data, reconcile balances, and draft notes. The finance owner keeps authority over unusual entries, estimates, write-offs, period locks, and final report release. Put that boundary in the calendar and the accounting permissions.

## Review the first cycles closely
Run the calendar through a small set of accounts and inspect whether the evidence is sufficient, questions arrive early, and reviewers can find open items. Adjust task wording after the first cycle, then expand only when the handoff is repeatable.

## Escalate by decision, not by volume
An exception should name the account, amount or document, issue, next action, owner, and due date. Escalate when a policy choice, approval, missing source, or potential misstatement blocks progress. A long list without a decision request is not a useful close packet.

## Hand off a signed summary
End the cycle with completed tasks, open exceptions, proposed entries awaiting approval, and the reviewer’s sign-off. The Philippines bookkeeper maintains the record; the business retains the final accounting judgment.
