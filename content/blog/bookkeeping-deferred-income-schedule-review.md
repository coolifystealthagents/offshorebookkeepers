## Define the schedule’s population

List the agreements or billing records included, the start and end dates, billed amount, recognized amount, and remaining balance. State whether the schedule covers all relevant contracts or only a defined subset.

## Tie releases to service periods

For each release, show the period, amount, source rationale, and journal reference. Compare the cumulative release to the amount billed and the portion still outstanding. A schedule should make the movement explainable, not merely produce a closing balance.

## Flag changes instead of rewriting history

Cancellations, renewals, scope changes, credits, and pauses should create visible exception notes. Preserve the prior schedule or a change record, then identify the approved treatment.

## Use a monthly review packet

The packet can include the source population, rollforward, new agreements, closed agreements, exception list, and reviewer sign-off. Remote support is strongest when the preparation boundary is explicit.

## Make the handoff reviewable

Keep the final packet with its source links, status, reviewer, and next review date. A clear preparation boundary lets Offshore Bookkeepers support recurring finance work while the business keeps control of approvals and accounting decisions.
