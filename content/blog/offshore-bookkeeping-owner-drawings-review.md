August 23, 2026 operating guide. This article is about bookkeeping preparation and review support. It is not accounting, tax, legal, payroll, or investment advice; client policy and authorized professionals control protected decisions.

## Define the transaction population

Owner drawings review should begin with the entities, bank accounts, equity or distribution accounts, card accounts, reporting period, and transaction types in scope. Include transfers, checks, card charges, reimbursements, and cash withdrawals when the procedure requires them. The bookkeeper can assemble the population from bank activity and the ledger. The owner, controller, CPA, or other authorized reviewer approves the scope because the treatment can depend on entity and tax context. Record excluded accounts and the extraction date. Without a complete population, a clean-looking owner account may simply reflect transactions that were never examined.

## Keep source facts unchanged

For each movement, record date, amount, account, payee or recipient, memo, bank reference, and ledger reference. Preserve the original bank description and source file. Put a proposed classification or question in a separate field. A bookkeeper can match a transfer to another account and identify a missing receipt. It should not relabel a personal item as business expense or decide whether a distribution is permitted. The reviewer needs to see the original evidence and the reasoning separately. That distinction is important when a familiar payee appears in several different contexts.

## Separate owner activity from business expense

Start with observable evidence, then ask the authorized owner to explain ambiguous movements. A payment to an owner, related party, or personal card may require a specific treatment under the business’s policy. The bookkeeper can flag the item and prepare a bounded question. It should not infer intent from a memo or prior classification. Keep reimbursements, advances, distributions, payroll, and operating expenses in distinct review states until the responsible person decides. A clean separation makes the ledger easier to review and prevents a recurring personal charge from becoming an unexamined business cost.

## Reconcile both sides of a transfer

When a movement appears to be a transfer, match the sending bank record with the receiving account or ledger entry. Check amount, date, currency, entity, and reference. A match does not prove the business purpose, so preserve the source and owner explanation. Investigate unmatched transfers, duplicate entries, intercompany movements, and payments that cross entities. The bookkeeper prepares the comparison. The authorized finance reviewer decides whether the result belongs in equity, payable, receivable, expense, or another account. Do not force the two sides to agree by editing one record without evidence.

## Make questions easy to answer

An escalation should show the item, sources checked, conflict, policy reference if one exists, decision needed, owner, and deadline. Ask whether a named payment was a distribution, reimbursement, advance, or business purchase rather than saying “please classify.” If the owner cannot provide support, record the response and next action. Silence is not approval. The bookkeeper can maintain reminders and update the queue. It should not close the item because the period is ending. A bounded question respects the owner’s context while keeping the preparation role from making an unsupported judgment.

## Protect sensitive account access

Use named accounts, multifactor authentication, and least privilege for banking and accounting systems. A bookkeeper should not change bank permissions, create an equity account, approve a personal reimbursement, or post an entry it originated without separate review. Do not share credentials to obtain a statement. Store bank records and identity information in the approved workspace. Record temporary access and remove it when the task ends. Owner transactions can be sensitive even when the amounts are ordinary. Access discipline keeps the evidence trustworthy and reduces the chance that an unexplained movement is altered before review.

## Review patterns carefully

Group open questions by account, owner, vendor, entity, and recurring date. Patterns can show a missing policy, a card that needs a clearer statement, or a transfer process that does not identify both sides. They do not establish tax treatment or business purpose by themselves. Sample ordinary and unusual items to test source links, owner explanations, and reviewer decisions. Track rework, unresolved items, and age. Do not reward a preparer for clearing questions without support. A small accurate register gives the finance lead a better basis for deciding what needs attention.

## Use the reporting packet as a cross-check

Compare the owner-transaction register with cash flow reports, equity activity, related-party balances, and the monthly review packet. This comparison finds omissions; it does not replace source evidence. A bookkeeper can identify that a movement appears in one report but not another and state the difference in plain language. The reviewer decides whether the reports use the same entity, period, and account scope. Retain report dates and filters. This check is useful after a bank feed reconnect, chart-of-accounts change, or entity transfer because those events can create a plausible-looking mismatch.

## Revisit unusual balances

An owner balance that grows, changes direction, or crosses entities deserves a fresh question even when a similar item was accepted before. The bookkeeper can compare periods, list the movement, and attach the supporting records. The authorized reviewer decides whether the pattern needs owner confirmation, a policy review, or professional advice. Do not use prior approval as automatic approval for a new fact pattern. Record the reason for reopening the item and keep the prior decision available. This approach gives the finance lead context without asking the preparation role to interpret a tax or equity consequence.

## Retain the approved outcome

Keep the scoped population, bank evidence, ledger reports, explanations, exception notes, reviewer decision, and posted reference. Preserve the original workpaper and add later corrections as history. Update the procedure when entities, bank accounts, approval rules, or reporting needs change. Offshore bookkeeping support can keep owner-drawings review organized without deciding tax or equity policy. The durable record shows which movements were explained, which remain open, and who accepted the final treatment.
