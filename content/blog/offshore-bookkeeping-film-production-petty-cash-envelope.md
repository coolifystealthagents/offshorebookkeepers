## Scope and publication note

Published October 5, 2026. This workflow supports bookkeeping preparation and review. It is not accounting, tax, legal, payroll, employment, or production-finance advice.

## Petty cash behaves differently on a set

A film shoot can move money through location scouts, art buyers, drivers, and production assistants before the accounting team sees a single receipt. The cash is physical, custody changes quickly, and a purchase may be urgent without being undocumented. Treating the bank withdrawal as the expense loses who held the money, which unit used it, and whether unused cash came back. A better design makes every envelope a small, time-bound subledger with an opening amount, named custodian, approved purpose, documented activity, and closing return.

The control begins before funds leave the production office. Give the envelope a unique ID that follows it through issue, replenishment, transfer, and wrap. Record production, episode or job, department, custodian, issue date, approved ceiling, denomination count, and approving manager. The custodian acknowledges the count. If one person hands the envelope to another, log both people and the time; do not silently overwrite the original name. That custody chain is as important as the final expense coding.

## Design a field packet people will actually use

Crew members need a simple capture routine that works from a phone. Each receipt image should show the entire document and be attached to an entry with envelope ID, purchase date, vendor, amount, tax, tip if allowed, department, job code, business purpose, and purchaser. Number the physical receipts in the same sequence. Where a vendor supplies only a handwritten ticket, capture the vendor name and what was bought while the event is still fresh. Sensitive personal information should not be added to a general expense folder.

The IRS explains why businesses need supporting documents for purchases and expenses in its [recordkeeping guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping). The Department of Labor separately describes payroll recordkeeping in its [FLSA fact sheet](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/21-flsa-recordkeeping). Neither source creates a film-production petty-cash policy. Use them as background, then follow the production entity's approved retention, expense, labor, and reimbursement rules.

## Reconcile before the next top-up

For each envelope, calculate opening cash plus approved replenishments, less supported purchases, less transfers out, and less cash returned. The result should equal cash physically counted. Reperform the addition from the receipt images rather than accepting a handwritten cover sheet total. Compare receipt dates with shoot dates and call sheets, and compare department or job codes with the current coding map. Keep sales tax, refundable deposits, expendables, rentals, meals, mileage, and employee reimbursements separate when the approved chart requires it.

Replenishment should not erase history. Close one numbered settlement and open the next, carrying only the verified physical balance forward. The register should show gross purchases even if the production office issues only the net amount needed to restore the ceiling. Otherwise a recurring shortage can disappear inside successive top-ups. A preparer may flag an out-of-policy purchase; only the authorized manager decides whether it is accepted, recovered, reclassified, or escalated.

## Work a realistic envelope difference

Suppose envelope ART-017 opens at $1,500. Its cover sheet lists $1,126.40 of purchases and a $373.60 cash return. The receipt images total $1,083.90 because a $42.50 hardware receipt is absent. Do not force the receipt total to the cover sheet. Record a $42.50 evidence exception, identify the purchaser from the line entry, and request the original or vendor duplicate. The physical envelope still balances, but evidence completeness does not. Those are two different conclusions and both belong in the review summary.

Now assume a $200 location key deposit appears among the receipts. If it is expected to come back, track it by counterparty and expected recovery date rather than burying it in location expense. When the key is returned, match the refund to that item. If the location manager retains part of the deposit for damage, attach the settlement explanation and route the coding decision. This approach makes the eventual refund intelligible even if it arrives after wrap.

## Use exceptions that lead to action

Useful exception types include missing receipt, illegible image, duplicate receipt, personal-looking purchase, date outside the production window, unapproved category, incorrect job code, deposit still open, cash shortage, cash overage, and custody gap. Give each item an amount, owner, due date, age, source link, and exact requested action. “Need backup” is weak; “Upload the $42.50 hardware receipt for ART-017 or provide the manager's exception decision by October 7” is operational.

Do not treat a manager's chat reaction as durable approval. Save the decision in the authorized system with the envelope record. Preserve the first submission and any corrected version, including who changed the code and why. The bookkeeper can assemble facts, reperform arithmetic, and draft proposed coding. The production accountant or authorized finance owner retains policy interpretation, approval, recovery from a worker, tax treatment, and final posting authority.

## Wrap by custodian as well as account

Before final wrap, filter the register by envelope status and named custodian. Every envelope should be returned, formally transferred, or covered by a documented exception. Then reconcile the combined envelope register to cash withdrawals, approved replenishments, returned bank deposits, and ledger entries. Trace a sample from the ledger back to the receipt and from issued cash forward to its final settlement. A zero cash-on-hand balance does not prove the expenses were supported or correctly classified.

Archive the issue acknowledgments, receipt index, physical counts, transfers, settlement sheets, exception decisions, deposit follow-ups, and final ledger tie-out under a consistent production-period label. Report open value separately from closed value, and carry unresolved deposits or shortages into a named post-wrap queue.
