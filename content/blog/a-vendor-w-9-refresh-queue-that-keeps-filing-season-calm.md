January arrives, the 1099 work starts, and four vendors have no current W-9. Two changed their legal name, one has a tax identifier that does not match the payments, and one was paid through a platform that already files. The filing is delayed by records that could have been refreshed in October.

A W-9 refresh queue is a short list with a deadline. It turns a January scramble into a calm October routine.

## Build the list from the vendor master

Start with every vendor paid during the year and flag the ones with a missing form, an old form, or a name that does not match the payment record.

Record the payment total beside each name. A vendor paid twice matters less than a vendor paid every month, and the list should show the difference.

## Check the legal name and identifier

Compare the W-9 legal name and tax identifier with the vendor master and the payment records. A mismatch is the most common cause of a rejected or corrected filing.

Do not assume the trade name is the legal name. Record the name exactly as it appears on the form.

## Request from a real contact

Send the request to the person who owns the vendor relationship, with a short explanation of why the form is needed. A message to a generic billing inbox often goes unanswered.

Record the request date and the contact. A second request should go to the same person, not a new address.

## Separate the platform vendors

Payments made through a payment platform or a staffing agency may be reported by that platform. Record the arrangement so the vendor is not asked for a form it does not need.

This step prevents duplicate requests and duplicate filings, which are harder to unwind than a missing form.

## Track the queue weekly

Review the open requests once a week and note the age of each. Add a backup contact when a request passes two weeks.

A short weekly review keeps the queue from becoming a December list. It also shows whether one vendor is blocking several payments.

## Record the refresh, not only the receipt

When a form arrives, update the vendor master, note the date, and keep the prior version. The refresh date tells next year's reviewer where to start.

A file with a form but no date is a form of unknown age. The date is part of the record.

## Handle the refusal

If a vendor will not provide a form, record the refusal and the decision about backup withholding. Do not leave the field empty without a note.

The decision belongs to the business owner or the tax adviser, not to the bookkeeper alone. The queue should show that the question was raised.

## A worked example

An October review finds 60 paid vendors. Eight have no current form, three have a name mismatch, and five are platform payments.

The bookkeeper requests the eight forms, corrects the three names after verification, and marks the five platforms as reported elsewhere. By January, the filing list is clean and the exceptions are documented.

## What the queue cannot do

The queue cannot verify a tax identifier with the IRS or decide the correct filing. It organizes the collection and the evidence.

Used before year-end, it prevents the common delay without overstating what a bookkeeper can confirm.
