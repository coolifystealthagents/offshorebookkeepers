## Test a reimbursement before posting
For a $186 travel claim, verify claimant, business purpose, date, receipt, currency, project, and approver. If the receipt shows $186 but the card statement shows $168, record the difference and ask whether tax, tip, or a duplicate explains it. The Philippines bookkeeper can prepare the exception and code a documented item; the manager decides policy exceptions and approval.

## Keep the review focused
Sample claims by employee, amount, and exception type. Compare the claim to the ledger posting and reimbursement batch, then preserve the reviewer’s decision. A control that checks only whether a file exists will miss duplicate claims, personal items, or claims submitted after a project closed.
Define claimant, business purpose, date, vendor, currency, amount, project or department, receipt, and approver fields. A Philippines bookkeeper can perform a consistent completeness check when the rule is explicit.

## Check policy before coding
Look for duplicate claims, personal items, split transactions, out-of-period dates, and missing approval. A policy flag is a question for the owner, not proof that a claim should be rejected or paid.

## Keep approval visible
Record who approved, when, what amount was approved, and any exception granted. The bookkeeper may route and remind, but should not approve a claim they prepared.

## Prepare payment safely
A payment-ready list should tie each claim to the approved packet and show total, payee, due date, and bank or payroll route. A separate authorized user verifies and releases the batch.

## Reconcile the result
Compare approved claims with the bank, payroll clearing, or accounts payable record. Investigate duplicates, unpaid approved items, and payments that do not match the approved amount.

## Report recurring gaps
Summarize missing receipts, late approvals, repeated policy exceptions, and coding corrections by owner or department. This improves the process without asking the offshore role to make policy.
