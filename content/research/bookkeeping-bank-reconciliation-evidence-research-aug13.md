## Research question
Bank reconciliation is often summarized as a difference of zero. That is necessary but not sufficient evidence. This study treats each bank account and statement period as a population containing deposits, withdrawals, transfers, fees, returns, and outstanding items. It examines whether each difference has an explanation and a path to resolution.

## Evidence model
Retain statement period, account identifier, opening and closing balance, ledger balance, reconciliation date, preparer, reviewer, and reconciliation difference. For every outstanding item, record transaction date, amount, source, age, expected clearing date, and owner. Distinguish timing items from book adjustments and bank errors. A plug entry should never replace an explanation.

Completeness testing matters where feeds fail or transactions are posted manually. Compare statement pages, bank export totals, and ledger activity; inspect unusual gaps, duplicate imports, and reversed entries. Review the current period and prior three months for items older than the organization’s stated tolerance. Age is a prioritization signal, not a universal rule.

## Handoff and conclusion
Bookkeeping support can prepare the reconciliation, attach statements, match activity, and maintain aged exceptions. The owner should approve adjustments, investigate unexplained withdrawals, and control bank access. The conclusion is that a reconciliation is strongest when balance equality, transaction completeness, and aged-item accountability are shown separately.
