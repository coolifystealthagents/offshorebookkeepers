A bookkeeper leaves on a Friday. The new bookkeeper opens the file on Monday and finds a reconciliation in progress, three missing bank statements, and a client who expects a report by Wednesday. The handover note says "all up to date."

A bookkeeping handover note makes the state of the work visible. This method note describes what to record, how to review it, and what the note cannot prove.

## Start with the ledger state

Record the last closed period, the reconciliations completed, and the reconciliations in progress. Include the date of the last bank reconciliation for each account.

A handover that does not state the ledger position leaves the new bookkeeper guessing. The first task becomes an investigation, not a continuation.

## List the open items

List the missing documents, the questions waiting on the client, and the entries that need a decision. Add the deadline for each.

Record the owner and the next action. An open item without an owner becomes the new bookkeeper's problem by default.

## Record the client arrangements

Note the billing arrangement, the reporting deadlines, and the communication preferences. These are the facts that affect the work every month.

Keep the personal history out of the note. The new bookkeeper needs the operating facts, not the story of the relationship.

## What the note can show

The note can show that the handover was prepared and what it covered. It can show the open items and the deadlines.

It can support a review if a deadline is missed. The record shows whether the item was known and who owned it.

## What the note cannot show

The note cannot show that the new bookkeeper understood the work. A complete note can be read without the context that makes it useful.

It cannot show that the ledger is accurate. The handover describes the state; the reconciliations are the evidence.

It also cannot show that the client relationship transferred. That requires a conversation and, often, an introduction.

## Methodology and limits

This note is an operating design informed by public guidance on internal control, recordkeeping, and client operations. It does not report an empirical study or establish a handover standard.

The fields were tested against common handovers: a planned departure, a temporary cover, a client transfer, and an emergency absence. The design keeps the ledger state and the client state distinct.

## Include a conversation

A written note should be paired with a short conversation. The new bookkeeper can ask about the open items and the client's expectations.

Record the conversation and the questions raised. A handover with no questions may indicate that the note was not read closely.

## Access and passwords

The handover should include the access the new bookkeeper needs, requested through the proper channel. A shared password in a note is a security problem.

Record the access requested and the date granted. The first week should not be spent waiting for a login.

## Deadlines

List the client deadlines for the next two months. A handover that ignores the calendar produces a missed filing in the first month.

Record the deadline, the owner, and the status. The new bookkeeper should see what is due before it is overdue.

## Client introduction

Where the client agrees, arrange a short introduction. A client who knows the new name is more likely to respond to a request.

Record the introduction and the client's preference for contact. The relationship transfers through people, not documents.

## A worked example

A bookkeeper hands over a retail client. The note lists the last closed month, an open reconciliation, and a missing statement.

The new bookkeeper requests the statement, closes the reconciliation, and meets the client on a short call. The first month runs without a missed deadline.

## Review the handover after a month

Ask the new bookkeeper what was missing and what was unnecessary. A repeated gap is a template change.

Record the feedback and update the note. The next handover should be faster and more accurate.

## A worked example

A bookkeeper hands over three clients. The note lists the last closed period, two open reconciliations, and a missing statement for one client.

The new bookkeeper calls the client, requests the statement, and closes the reconciliation. The handover took one hour and prevented a missed deadline.

## Limits and appropriate use

This note is a method design, not an empirical study. It does not establish a handover checklist or a service level. Results depend on the honesty of the note and the quality of the records.

Small firms may hand over verbally, and the note should say so. The absence of a written record is a finding about continuity.

## Conclusion

A bookkeeping handover note shows the state of the ledger and the open items. It does not prove understanding or accuracy. Used with a conversation and a monthly review, it makes a change of bookkeeper survivable for the client.
