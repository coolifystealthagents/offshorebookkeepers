## Evidence scope
IRS guidance supplies general recordkeeping context, and COSO and GAO address documented responsibilities and review. PCAOB material is relevant as audit context but does not prescribe a collections workflow. The listed security and workforce sources do not establish a receivables-aging benchmark.

## What the evidence supports
Aging work becomes more reviewable when every exception has an owner, next action, and evidence trail. The handoff should map invoice status, last customer contact, dispute reason, promised date, and escalation owner without treating the age bucket alone as evidence of collectability.

## Measurement design
Measure unresolved items by age band, missing dispute evidence, unapplied cash, and handoff completeness. Define whether disputed and unapplied items remain in each denominator, because different treatments can make two aging reports incomparable.

## Operating boundary
A support team can prepare the aging, update documented contact status, and maintain an exception log. Credit decisions, concessions, write-offs, and customer commitments belong to the authorized business owner under the company’s policy.

## Methodology and limitations
The sources support general documentation and control principles but do not establish a single collection-performance benchmark or validate the suggested review cadence. Results depend on credit terms, dispute practices, posting cutoffs, and the treatment of unapplied cash.

## Source notes
The status fields and measures in this report are proposed operating controls. They should not be attributed to the workforce or country data in the source list, and they are not estimates of collection success.
