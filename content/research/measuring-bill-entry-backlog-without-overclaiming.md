A bill entry backlog is easy to see and easy to misread. The stack of invoices on the desk looks like slow work. Some of those invoices are waiting for approval, some are disputed, and some arrived yesterday. The count does not say which.
A bill entry backlog review separates the documents waiting for entry from the documents waiting for a decision. It is useful because payables depends on a clean intake. It is also easy to overclaim, because a backlog can be a timing difference rather than a failure.

## What the review can show

The review can show the number of documents waiting, the value, the age, and the intake channel. It can show the items waiting for entry, approval, or a dispute decision. It can show the oldest item and the owner.
The review can also show the pattern by vendor and by channel. A cluster from one email inbox may indicate a routing problem. A cluster from one vendor may indicate a recurring dispute.

## What the review cannot show

The review cannot show whether an unentered invoice will be paid late. It cannot show whether the delay matters to the vendor or the business. It also cannot show whether the entry team is working hard.
The review cannot prove that the process is understaffed. A backlog can come from a missing approval, a changed system, or a sudden volume increase. The reason has to be recorded before a cause is claimed.

## Methodology

We reviewed public guidance on internal control, security controls, and recordkeeping, and mapped it to a private bill entry process. The sources support documentation and verification discipline. They do not prescribe a backlog target or a staffing level.
We tested the model against four common cases: an invoice waiting for entry, an invoice waiting for approval, a disputed invoice, and an invoice entered but not matched to a purchase order. The proposed states are an operating design.

## Define the intake channels

List the channels through which bills arrive, such as email, mail, a vendor portal, and a shared drive. Record the channel for each item. A channel without an owner tends to become the backlog.
Assign one owner for each channel and a backup. The owner confirms that the documents are picked up and entered. A channel that depends on someone noticing is a channel that will fail during a busy week.

## Separate the waiting states

Use distinct states: received, entered, awaiting approval, disputed, and matched. A document is only in one state at a time. The state tells the reviewer where the work sits.
Record the date the item entered each state. A document that has been in "awaiting approval" for ten days is a different problem from one that was received yesterday. The age matters.

## Record the age and the value

Report the age in buckets and the value by state. A large count of small invoices is a different problem from a small count of large ones. The value helps the owner prioritize.
Keep the currency visible. A multi-currency backlog that is reported in one total hides the exposure in each currency. Record the original currency and the rate if a reporting currency is used.

## Chase the reason, not only the count

For each aged item, record why it is waiting. Common reasons include a missing purchase order, an unclear approval, a bank detail change, and a dispute. The reason points to the fix.
Do not assign a reason the owner did not state. A guessed reason leads to the wrong change. If the reason is unknown, record it as unknown and ask the owner what would help.

## Review the vendor pattern

Look at the vendors that appear most often in the backlog. A vendor with a recurring missing purchase order may need a different intake rule. A vendor with repeated disputes may need a contract review.
Share the pattern with the vendor owner rather than publishing a list. The conversation should produce one or two changes. A vendor conversation is more useful than another reminder.

## Protect invoice and bank information

The backlog includes invoice images, bank details, and vendor contacts. Keep them in the approved system and limit access to the people who process bills. Do not circulate a backlog list with bank details in a broad channel.
Set a retention period with the business's records policy. A backlog log kept indefinitely creates risk without adding insight. Keep enough to support the review and no more.

## Keep the sample honest

A review of one week and one entity is a check, not a benchmark. Say so in the summary. Include the invoice volume and the channels so the reader can judge the evidence.
A small sample can show a pattern but cannot support a percentage for every team. Repeat the review before a conclusion is treated as stable, and state the limits in the same document.

## Set a simple intake standard

Write a short standard for what a complete bill looks like: vendor, invoice number, amount, date, purchase order if required, and approval route. A bill that arrives without those elements is not ready for entry. Record the missing element and route it back through the approved channel. A clear standard reduces the backlog without adding staff.

## Review the backlog with the approvers

The approvers control a large part of the backlog. Share the aged items and the reasons with them, not only the entry team. A recurring approval delay may need a delegate or a threshold change. The conversation should produce one or two changes. Record the change and the date so the next review can judge whether it helped.

## Keep the log light enough to maintain

The backlog log should take a minute per document, not a full review. Record the channel, the state, the age, the value, and the reason. Leave the invoice detail in the accounting system. Review the fields after a month and remove any that nobody used. A light log that is kept every week is more useful than a detailed one that is abandoned. The review should ask whether the log changed a priority or a deadline. If it never did, the log is too heavy for the value it adds. A short dated note about the change is enough to show the improvement. Keep that note with the backlog report so the next review can compare. A small change that is written down becomes part of the routine. That is how a backlog review turns into a process.

## Bounded pilot

Review one week of bill intake for one entity. Record the channel, the state, the age, the value, and the reason. Report the backlog by state and vendor. Ask a reviewer who did not enter the bills to trace three items to their current state. If they cannot, the fields need work.

## Limitations

This brief describes an operating review model. It does not establish a performance standard, a legal obligation, or a measure of team productivity. The cited sources support general control and recordkeeping principles. Results depend on the completeness of the intake record and the honesty of the reason notes.

## Conclusion

A bill entry backlog shows documents waiting in different states. It cannot prove that payments will be late. Used with the age, the value, and the reason, it shows where the intake process breaks and which channel or approval needs a change.
