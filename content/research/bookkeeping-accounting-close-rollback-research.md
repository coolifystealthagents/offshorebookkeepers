## Research question
What can a posted-entry correction tell an owner about bookkeeping close quality? A reversal may reflect a genuine estimate update, a late document, a reviewer decision, or an avoidable coding error. Research must separate those causes before assigning responsibility to a remote preparer.

## Evidence scope and method
Public accounting, evidence, control, and recordkeeping sources frame the review. They do not publish an acceptable rollback rate for outsourced bookkeeping. Sample corrections by account and reason, then trace original entry, support, approver, correction, period impact, and whether the original remained visible. Compare like periods and transaction mix; do not rank teams by raw correction count.

## Role and risk analysis
A preparer may propose a correction and document the evidence. A reviewer or authorized owner should approve material or judgmental changes. The audit trail should retain both entries and the reason. In an offshore handoff, a clear rollback note prevents a time-zone gap from becoming an unexplained ledger change. The useful finding is the quality of the explanation, not merely the existence of an adjustment.

## Limitations and conclusion
Some corrections are inevitable in accrual accounting, and a clean period may reflect missing review rather than perfect work. System histories vary. The method cannot establish fraud or financial statement accuracy. The evidence-led conclusion is that correction research is meaningful when it classifies cause, authority, and retained support; a raw rollback count is not a quality benchmark.
