## Evidence scope
IRS recordkeeping guidance and COSO and GAO control principles support maintaining traceable financial records; NIST and CISA provide access-security context. The listed sources are not jurisdiction-specific trust-account rules and do not establish legal compliance. Governing bar rules and qualified legal review remain controlling.

## What the evidence supports
Trust-account support requires matter-level traceability and a conservative escalation boundary. Each item should connect the client ledger, bank activity, matter reference, authorization, reconciliation status, and dated reviewer sign-off without assuming that bookkeeping evidence resolves a legal question.

## Measurement design
Measure unreconciled items, missing matter references, stale balances, and review aging. Define the tested account and cutoff, and report unknown ownership separately; none of these measures is a legal-compliance rate.

## Operating boundary
A support role may organize reconciliations and evidence under written procedures. Authorized attorneys or other designated local professionals must retain trust decisions, transfers, exception disposition, and interpretation of the governing rules.

## Methodology and limitations
The public sources support general recordkeeping, access, and control concepts but do not validate a trust-account workflow or interpret any jurisdiction’s rules. A reconciled bank balance can still conceal a matter-level error, and the proposed measures cannot establish legal compliance.

## Source notes
The matter fields and role boundaries are conservative operating recommendations. They must be reconciled with the firm’s governing trust-account rules, banking terms, and approved procedures.
