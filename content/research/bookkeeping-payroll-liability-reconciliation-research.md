## Research question
Payroll close risk comes from confusing three dates: the work period, the payroll processing date, and the settlement date. This report studies the evidence needed to reconcile wages, employee deductions, employer taxes, benefits, and payments for one defined pay run. It does not provide tax advice or decide whether a worker is correctly classified.

## Evidence model
Start with the approved payroll register and record the pay period, processing date, entity, currency, and system version. Split gross wages, employee withholdings, employer liabilities, benefits, net pay, and fees. For each liability, retain the source calculation, the journal entry reference, the payment or filing reference, and the remaining open balance. A single “payroll clearing” total hides useful differences.

Reconcile the register to the bank payment by pay run, not merely by the day’s total bank activity. Then reconcile tax and benefit liabilities to remittance evidence or a documented open item. A payment in transit can explain a difference; it cannot be silently treated as settled. Report count and amount of open items by age, with the exact due-date convention used.

## Findings
Timing differences are ordinary, but unexplained differences are not. Compare the current run with the prior three runs for employee count, gross pay, deductions, employer burden, and manual adjustments. The comparison is a screening signal, not a performance benchmark. A large change may be a bonus, a new jurisdiction, a correction, or an error; the owner must decide which.

Access is part of the evidence boundary. The preparer needs the register and settlement records, while bank-release rights and tax-filing authority may belong elsewhere. Named access, retained approvals, and an exception log provide a review trail without giving one person unrestricted control over calculation, posting, and payment.

## Handoff and limitations
A bookkeeping team can prepare the reconciliation, identify missing remittances, and maintain the open-item schedule. The payroll or finance owner should approve unusual adjustments, interpret tax notices, resolve employee disputes, and confirm filings. The report cannot validate payroll law, benefits eligibility, or the accuracy of the underlying time record.

The conclusion is narrow: payroll evidence is strongest when each run has a defined period, category-level reconciliation, dated settlement status, and a named escalation owner. Keeping liability date separate from payment date produces a more honest close than forcing every difference to zero.
