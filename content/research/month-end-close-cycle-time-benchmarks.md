## Evidence scope
IRS guidance provides recordkeeping context, COSO and GAO support documented control responsibility, and PCAOB material provides audit context for evidence and review. None of the listed sources publishes a universal small-business close time. Security, fraud, workforce, and country data cannot supply that missing benchmark.

## What the evidence supports
A close can be measured as a sequence of owned handoffs rather than a single finish date. Each task should identify its required input, preparer, evidence, reviewer, due event, and completion rule so elapsed time can be assigned to the relevant stage.

## Measurement design
Split cycle time into preparation, exception resolution, review, and sign-off. Report late inputs, aged exceptions, rework, and reviewer touches alongside duration; otherwise a faster preparation stage can conceal a growing approval queue.

## Operating boundary
A support team can prepare schedules and maintain the close log. Designated finance owners should approve estimates, policy choices, material adjustments, payment actions, and final reporting. Establish a local baseline before changing staffing or targets.

## Methodology and limitations
The sources support recordkeeping and control concepts but do not validate the proposed dashboard or target. Observed cycle time depends on system count, transaction mix, late source data, materiality, reviewer policy, and the definition of completion, so cross-company comparisons are not supported here.

## Source notes
The stage definitions and dashboard fields are proposed operating measures, not close-time findings reported by the listed sources.
