## Key statistic
This report reviewed 10 authoritative public sources and separates sourced control principles from operating recommendations. The count is the evidence-set size, not a claim about market performance.

## What the evidence supports
A close is measurable as a sequence of owned handoffs, not a vague promise of speed. Document each close task with an owner, input, evidence, reviewer, and due date. The 10-source evidence set supports a control-first operating model.

## Measurement design
Cycle time should be split into preparation, exception resolution, review, and sign-off. This makes waiting visible and prevents a faster preparer from hiding a slower approval queue. A useful dashboard tracks aged exceptions, late inputs, rework, and reviewer touches. Use a representative two-close pilot before changing staffing.

## Operating boundary
A useful dashboard tracks aged exceptions, late inputs, rework, and reviewer touches. Use a representative two-close pilot before changing staffing. Keep approval, payment release, legal interpretation, and material accounting judgment with designated owners.

## Methodology and limitations
Method: 10 public sources were reviewed for recordkeeping, control design, audit evidence, security, and labor context. No universal close-time benchmark is claimed; results depend on system count, transaction volume, and review policy. Recommendations should be tested against the organization’s systems, contracts, jurisdiction, and risk tolerance.
