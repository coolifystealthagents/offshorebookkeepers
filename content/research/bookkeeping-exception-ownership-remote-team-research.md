## Research question
How should a distributed bookkeeping team assign ownership when a transaction cannot be completed on first review? The report treats exceptions as decision queues rather than as a measure of individual failure.

## Methodology
COSO and GAO support responsibility and control activities; PCAOB describes the importance of evidence and review; NIST contributes continuity thinking for unresolved work. The proposed model classifies an exception as missing evidence, data mismatch, system issue, or accounting judgment. Each class receives an owner, due event, escalation route, and closure evidence.

## Why ownership fails
Remote handoffs often use a shared queue with no named next actor. “Waiting on client” can remain open after the client supplied a partial answer. “Needs review” can include both a simple coding question and a material estimate. A label without a decision owner makes age hard to interpret and encourages duplicate requests.

## Operating implications
The preparer should document what is missing and propose the next evidence request. The reviewer should decide whether the item can be posted, held, or escalated. Client owners resolve policy, approval, and judgment questions. A dated status history lets a manager distinguish slow response from repeated rework.

## Limitations and conclusion
Public frameworks do not prescribe an exception taxonomy for bookkeeping teams, and the model cannot set materiality for a client. The evidence supports a narrow conclusion: remote bookkeeping work becomes more controllable when every unresolved item has a role owner, a decision event, and closure evidence. The system should make those fields visible without claiming that every exception can be automated.

## Source notes
This is an operating-control model. It does not replace a client's accounting policy or approval matrix.
