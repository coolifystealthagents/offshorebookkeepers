The payroll journal matches the payroll report to the cent. The bank withdrawal matches. The reconciliation is clean, and the benefits expense for the quarter is still wrong because the employer contribution was posted to wages.

A payroll journal reconciliation compares totals. This brief separates the total, the components, and the classification, and explains what the reconciliation can support.

## Define what is compared

Record the payroll report, the journal entry, and the bank withdrawal. State which totals are compared and at what level.

A reconciliation that compares only the net pay total can miss a classification error in the gross and employer portions.

## Separate the components

Break the journal into gross wages, employer taxes, employee deductions, benefits, and net pay. Reconcile each component to the payroll report.

The component view is where mistakes appear. A total that matches can still have wages and benefits swapped.

## What the reconciliation can show

The reconciliation can show that the journal and the payroll report agree and that the bank withdrawal matches the net pay.

It can show the pay runs where the totals differ and the accounts that need correction. That is the core control.

## What the reconciliation cannot show

The reconciliation cannot show that the employee was paid correctly, that the tax was calculated correctly, or that the department coding is right.

It also cannot show that the payroll liability accounts were cleared. That requires a separate balance review.

## Check the classification

Sample the journal lines and compare the account to the chart of accounts and the payroll policy. Record the department, the location, and the pay type.

A misclassified line can pass every total check and still distort the management report.

## Review the liability accounts

Reconcile the payroll tax and benefit liabilities to the provider statements each month. Record the balance and the clearing date.

An uncleared liability is a cash risk that the payroll journal cannot reveal.

## Keep the evidence

Store the payroll report, the journal, the bank confirmation, and the provider statements together. Record the reviewer and the date.

The evidence should let a new accountant repeat the reconciliation without asking for files.

## A worked example

A payroll journal matches the report at 84,200 dollars net. The component check finds 6,100 dollars of employer benefit contribution posted to wages.

The total was right and the classification was wrong. The correction moves the amount and updates the department report for the quarter.

## Method note and limits

This brief is an operating design informed by public guidance on internal control and recordkeeping. It does not report an empirical study or give tax advice.

The fields were tested against common payroll patterns: a salaried run, an hourly run, a tipped payroll, and a multi-state run. The design keeps total, component, and classification separate.

## Conclusion

A payroll journal reconciliation proves the totals agree, not that the payroll is correct. Reconciling the components and reviewing the classification turns the check into a real control.
