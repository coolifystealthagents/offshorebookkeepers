Published July 28, 2026; updated September 17, 2026. This page intentionally reports no offshore price range. Public occupational and compensation sources do not make differently scoped vendor offers comparable, so the defensible output here is a cost-comparison method rather than a market estimate.

## Fix the work package before requesting prices

Write one schedule of recurring tasks, entities, ledgers, transaction volumes, systems, cutoff times, deliverables, exception ownership, and review responsibilities. State expected capacity and seasonal peaks. Ask each option to price that same package and identify exclusions.

Use the BLS occupational description as a scope check, not a rate card: transaction entry, records, reports, and reconciliation work differ from policy decisions, final accounting judgments, payment release, and controller review. If one quote includes managed review and another supplies preparation capacity only, their headline prices do not represent the same service.

## Separate the cost components

Build distinct rows for direct wages or vendor fees; employee benefits where applicable; client-side management and review; recruiting or vendor management; software seats; secure access and audit logging; onboarding; process documentation; transition overlap; business continuity; and correction work. Keep one-time and recurring amounts separate. Record currency, tax treatment, exchange-rate convention, source, quote date, validity period, and every quantity assumption.

The BLS compensation notes support separating wages from employer benefit costs in an employee comparison. NIST's control catalog identifies access, logging, and continuity activities that may create implementation or administration work. Neither source supplies an offshore price, a vendor markup, or the cost of a particular control environment.

## Compare three operating arrangements consistently

An individual contractor, a staffing arrangement, and a managed service allocate supervision and delivery responsibility differently. Model the actual allocation instead of attaching a generic percentage. For each option, identify who trains the preparer, provides backup coverage, reviews work, administers access, investigates exceptions, updates procedures, and bears the cost of replacement or idle capacity.

Keep authorized finance decisions outside a nominal bookkeeping package unless the proposal explicitly names qualified responsibility and controls. Payment approval, bank administration, accounting policy, tax positions, and material close judgments should not be assumed from a broad service label.

## Turn the model into comparable units

Choose units that match the decision: monthly cost for the defined package, cost per completed recurring process, or cost per accepted deliverable. The denominator must apply the same acceptance rule to every option. A processed transaction is not equivalent to a reviewed and accepted reconciliation.

Show a base case and separately labeled scenarios for volume, exchange rate, overtime, turnover, and rework. Do not hide uncertain inputs in one total. The formula, source date, and assumption owner should remain visible so a buyer can refresh the model when a quote or operating condition changes.

## Replace assumptions with a paid pilot

A bounded pilot should use representative work and a written acceptance rule. Record actual preparer hours, client review hours, access setup, questions, returned items, corrections, and elapsed time. Reprice the model from those observations without extrapolating beyond comparable tasks or periods.

A pilot can reveal that an estimate was incomplete; it cannot prove how a full-scale arrangement will perform or that one staffing model caused an observed result. Volume mix, learning, reviewer availability, and system access remain plausible explanations.

## Reporting limits

This method does not provide a 2026 price, salary conversion, country premium, savings percentage, or universal low-cost option. The cited BLS pages are U.S. labor references, and NIST is a control catalog rather than a pricing publication. A final decision record should therefore attach the dated proposals and local inputs actually used, disclose excluded costs, and identify who approved the scope and assumptions.
