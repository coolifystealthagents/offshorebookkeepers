## What the benchmark measures
This benchmark is a decision framework, not a universal price list. Offshore bookkeeping offers can cover very different work, from transaction coding to a fully managed close. A useful comparison starts with the same ledger count, transaction volume, systems, hours, deliverables, and review boundary.

## Key stats and cost inputs
Total operating cost includes direct labor or vendor fees, client-side review, software seats, secure access, onboarding, documentation, and expected rework. Exchange rates and labor-market conditions change, so a quote should carry a date and a written scope.

## Compare three operating models
An individual contractor can be flexible but may require more direct management. A staffing arrangement adds recruiting and workforce support while the client owns the process. A managed service usually owns a defined output and review layer. None is automatically best; the right model follows the control and supervision the business can provide.

## Build a like-for-like cost table
Create one row for each recurring process and columns for volume, frequency, preparer, reviewer, turnaround, systems, evidence, and exception owner. Price every option against that table. Keep payment release, bank administration, and final accounting judgments with authorized finance owners.

## Validate estimates with a pilot
A two-to-four-week paid pilot can test cycle time, exception quality, communication, and reviewer effort. Begin with representative but limited work, record every correction, and compare the measured result with the assumptions used in the cost model.

## Method and limitations
The cited public sources establish labor, recordkeeping, security, and control context. They do not publish one universal offshore-bookkeeping price. Actual pricing depends on scope, location, seniority, vendor model, exchange rates, and service levels. Recheck changeable figures when using this framework for a buying decision.
